# Level Up Your Play: outlines for the remaining eight tutorials

What each tutorial needs to cover, taken from the Comprehensive Rules. Section names in brackets point to where the rule lives. Items marked **(recap)** were already shown in an earlier tutorial and only need a short reminder.

---

## 1. Traits and Actions: timing and limitations

**The idea:** a card's text does things in two ways. A *trait* just happens or is just true. An *action* is something a player chooses to do, at a particular moment.

- **Ability versus action.** An ability is the printed text on a card or player. An action is the process that starts at a point in time, and it takes the keywords and designator of its ability.
- **Traits.**
  - Continuous traits are simply true while their condition holds ("This card has +2F while bowed").
  - Triggered traits fire on a trigger ("After this Personality enters play: ...") and are mandatory.
  - A trait can work while its card is bowed. Abilities cannot.
- **Designators**, which say when an action can be taken:
  - Limited: at an opportunity to act in your own Action Phase.
  - Open: at an opportunity to act in any player's Action Phase.
  - Battle: at an opportunity to act in the Combat Segment.
  - Reaction: at its stated trigger.
  - Interrupt: a Reaction. It is taken in the Reaction window that fits its text, usually "before an action's effects resolve". *(Mendon's ruling; the Comprehensive Rules list it as its own designator.)*
  - Engage: a Reaction taken at engagement, when a battlefield is engaged. Printed on a Personality or attachment, the card must be present at that battlefield to use it. Regions, Holdings and Strongholds are not units: their own text names the battlefield they act on. Home, Absent and the listed exceptions are exempt (R-064). *(Mendon's ruling.)*
  - An ability with two designators (Open/Battle) counts as having both, but the action it creates has only the one that matches when it was taken.
- **Action keywords** (Political, Tactical, Ninja, and so on) belong to the action, not just the card. Some cards look for them. **(recap Tactician from Keywords 1)**
- **Action rounds.** Players alternate taking actions or passing. When every player passes in succession, the phase or segment ends. A pass is not permanent: a new action reopens it.
- **Limitations on using abilities.**
  - Once per turn unless the text says otherwise ("twice per turn", "once per game").
  - Never from a bowed card, unless the text says "even if bowed".
  - Never from a card that is out of play, in a resolution area or in a focusing area.
  - Always voluntary. A single copy of a Reaction cannot be taken twice to the same trigger.
  - If an ability depends on a condition, using it counts as the turn's use even if the condition lapses and returns.
- **Using an ability, step by step:**
  - A: announce the action.
  - B: choose performers.
  - C: required targeting.
  - D: pay costs.
  - E: action resolution.
  - F: the action ends.
- **Performing.** The performer is the Personality (or card) doing the action. Some actions are performed by the player, not a card. Order Seppuku is the example: the Personality does not perform it. **(recap Seppuku)**
- **Additional actions.** A card can grant an extra action, taken at a later time than the one that granted it. **(recap Wateru in Keywords 2)**

## 2. Cost and Effect: paying versus applying

**The idea:** a cost is what you must do to be allowed to act. An effect is what happens because you acted. Costs are all-or-nothing. Effects can partly fail.

- **Definitions.**
  - A cost is usually a change to the state or stats of a card, a player or something else.
  - An effect is anything else that creates, changes, restricts or permits something.
  - Targeting is neither a cost nor an effect.
- **Costs must fully happen.**
  - A card already in the end state cannot pay the cost. A dishonorable Personality cannot pay "Dishonor one of your Personalities".
  - A cost that is prevented, negated or delayed is not paid. A player who cannot lose Honor cannot pay "Lose 2 Honor".
  - A stat cost must change the stat by the full amount. A card with 1C cannot pay "Give -2C".
  - You may not pay a cost by changing another player's cards or tokens.
- **Kinds of cost.**
  - Mandatory (the default). These are the Gold Cost of a card entering play, and the text before the colon.
  - Alternative: choose one ("Bow your Stronghold or discard the Imperial Favor") and you cannot pay both.
  - Conditional ("Bow this Shugenja unless he is Fire"). "Unless" comes after the cost. "If" before the cost is a restriction on using the action at all.
  - Optional ("you may also pay 4 Gold", "may ... as a cost"). If you skip it, only its associated effects do not happen.
- **Effects.**
  - Each individual change is a separate effect. "Bow two Samurai and gain 2 Honor" is three effects.
  - Effects occur in the order written. Modifying phrases apply at the same moment as the effect they modify.
  - Several effects from one piece of text happen simultaneously unless it says "consecutively".
  - Battles, phases and additional actions an effect grants are delayed until the action has ended.
  - An effect "on" a card changes its stats, keywords, abilities, tokens, or its use of them.
- **Effects that change other effects**, briefly. Negation, prevention, substitution ("instead") and delay. The detailed order is in Reactions.
- **Gold.** Paying Gold is a cost. A player can pay from Holdings and Strongholds. **(recap Recruiting)**
- **Pairs to show side by side.**
  - A cost that cannot be paid, so nothing happens.
  - An effect with no legal target, so only that part is skipped.

## 3. Special Actions: Attacks (Ranged and Melee)

**The idea:** a Ranged or Melee Attack is a card effect that kills one unit at a time during a battle. It is not the battle's own fighting.

- **What it is.** "Ranged X Attack" means: you may target a Follower, or a Personality without Followers, in the current enemy army. If its Force is equal to or less than X, destroy it. A Melee Attack works identically. The only difference is the name: actions and traits that affect one affect the other only if they say so explicitly.
- **When it applies.** In a battle, as part of a Battle action. It comes from the card that creates it, not from the rulebook.
- **Strength.** X is a stat, so it takes bonuses, penalties and minimums.
- **Targeting.**
  - Only enemy units in the current army count.
  - A Personality with Followers cannot be the target. His Follower can.
  - Targeting happens during action resolution, so a lack of targets does not stop you announcing the action.
  - If targeting is changed to another card type, the attack can still only destroy a Personality or Follower.
- **Comparing Force.** Equal Force is destroyed. One more is not. Modified Force at the time of resolution is what counts.
- **Multiple attacks.** Simultaneous by default: targeted together, then applied together. Consecutive attacks apply to each target before choosing the next.
- **What it does and does not do.** It only destroys. It does not bow, move or dishonor. Removing a unit before battle resolution can change who wins.
- **Worth showing.** An attack that destroys, one that fails on Force, and one that cannot target a Personality with a Follower.

## 4. Special Actions: Duels

**The idea:** a duel is a one-on-one contest decided by focused cards, not by the armies.

- **Challenge, then duel.**
  - A challenge names a challenger (and its controller) and a challenged Personality (and its controller).
  - "X duels Y" means "X issues a challenge to Y".
  - A challenge does not happen if both Personalities have the same controller, or if either card is not a Personality.
  - Challenges are unrefusable unless the effect allows refusing. If refused, the challenge ends and the refusal consequences apply.
  - If a Personality leaves play, the duel ends without resolution, and unresolved Focus Effects do not resolve.
- **The duel, in order:**
  - A: the duel begins.
  - B: each player has a focusing area, which only they may look at.
  - C: the focusing round.
    - The challenged player may focus or strike first, then they alternate.
    - To focus, take a card from your hand or the top of your deck, face down.
    - A player who has focused four times must strike. There is no "pass".
  - D: reveal the focused cards.
  - E: Focus Effects resolve, in an order the active player chooses.
  - F: resolution begins.
  - G: total scores.
  - H: determine the outcome.
  - I: consequences.
- **Scores.**
  - A Personality's duel stat (Chi by default) plus the modified Focus Values of his focused cards.
  - A "duel of Force" uses Force instead.
- **Outcome.**
  - Higher score wins.
  - A tie means no winner and both lose.
  - There is no default consequence. The card that made the challenge says what winning and losing do.
  - If both players win or both lose through effects, the card text says what follows.
- **Focus Value and Focus Effects.** Every card has a Focus Value. A Focus Effect is a trait on a focused card that resolves in step E. Focusing is not playing the card.
- **Refusing.** Show a card that allows refusal and what it costs.
- **Duelist (recap, with the new detail).**
  - A tie against a non-Duelist is won by the Duelist.
  - Before Focus Effects resolve, the Duelist's controller may discard a focused card and focus another. If both are Duelists, the active player goes first.
- **Iaijutsu.** A descriptive keyword. It carries no rules of its own; the card's text does the work.

## 5. Special Actions: Dishonor

**The idea:** a Personality can be honorable or dishonorable, and a dishonorable Personality is a risk to your Honor.

- **Two states.**
  - Honorable is normal.
  - Dishonorable is shown by turning the card upside down. If the card is also bowed, its top faces left.
  - Only Personalities have the state. It stays with a face-up card out of play.
- **Vocabulary.** Dishonor is the act of changing honorable to dishonorable. Rehonor is the reverse. Dishonored and rehonored are the past tenses.
- **Effects of being dishonorable.** A dishonorable Personality's Personal Honor can be at most 0.
- **Dying dishonorable.**
  - When a dishonorable Personality is destroyed, his controller loses Honor equal to his base Personal Honor. This is a rulebook effect, not a card effect.
  - "Dishonorable dead" and "honorable dead" are the two states of a dead Personality.
- **Ways to rehonor.** All three are substitutions for gaining Honor.
  - Gaining Honor from an action or trait that targeted, came from or was performed by a dishonorable Personality of yours rehonors him instead.
  - Gaining Honor from attaching a card to him rehonors him instead.
  - Gaining Honor from destroying enemy cards in battle resolution rehonors every dishonorable Personality in the winning army instead. In a tie they are rehonored before being destroyed.
  - If the rehonoring fails, the Honor is still not gained.
- **Seppuku (recap, from Keywords 1).**
  - An Open action that targets a dishonorable Courtier, Samurai or Shugenja you control.
  - He is rehonored, then destroyed, so you lose no Honor.
  - It is repeatable, and the Personality is not the performer.
- **Dishonor as a cost.** A dishonorable Personality cannot pay a cost of dishonoring him.
- **Losing by Dishonor.** Family Honor of -20 or below makes you lose at the start of your next End Phase, even if you recover to -19 or higher in the meantime. The last player standing wins a Dishonor Victory.
- **Entering-play Honor loss.** Many Personalities cost Honor to bring in. Show how it can push a player toward this loss. **(recap Takashi and Nitoshi)**

## 6. Rings and Enlightenment

**The idea:** Rings are put into play by meeting a condition, and five different ones win the game.

- **What a Ring is.**
  - A card type with a blue-black background.
  - It represents one of the five elements (Air, Earth, Fire, Void, Water) through its element keyword.
  - Rings are Unique. **(recap Unique)**
- **How a Ring enters play.** Not bought with Gold. Each Ring has its own "Play after..." condition in its text, and you can then play it from your hand. The in-game Ring cards are the source, not the older printings quoted in the Comprehensive Rules (those differ in conditions and abilities):
  - Ring of Air: play after you resolve two or more Favor actions in one turn.
  - Ring of Earth: play after a battle resolves at your own Province, if it was not destroyed and enemy units were ever at its battlefield.
  - Ring of Fire: play after you win a duel during a battle, if your Personality did not enter the duel with higher Chi than the other.
  - Ring of the Void: play only if you have two or fewer Rings in play, and discard your hand.
  - Ring of Water: play after a battle resolves in which you played a Terrain, destroyed a Terrain, and destroyed any cards or Provinces during resolution.
  - Each Ring also says you may discard it from your hand to use its ability without cost.
- **Using a Ring.** Each Ring has an ability, usually bowing it, and most are Repeatable. Out of play, you may only choose the discard cost, never the bow cost. In play, the abilities are:
  - Air: straighten your target Personality or attachment (Battle or Open).
  - Earth: the Battle action cannot move Personalities (Interrupt).
  - Fire: target an enemy card without attachments; if you lost the battle, destroy it after the battle resolves (Battle).
  - Void: draw a card; if you now have more cards than any other player, discard one (Limited).
  - Water: move your target Personality home or, if he would be opposed, to the current battlefield (Battle, Absent).
- **Enlightenment Victory.**
  - After the start of one of your turns, if you have five Rings in play with five different elements.
  - Each Ring must have last entered play by its own text. A Ring put into play by another effect does not count.
  - It is no longer an immediate win.
  - Cards that say they do not count towards an Enlightenment Victory do not count. Every False Ring says so (it carries Fudo), and each costs 2 Honor to put into play. **(show one False Ring and why it does not count)**
- **Rings in play** are cards, so they can be targeted, bowed or destroyed.
- **Worth showing.** A Ring entering play on its trigger, using its ability, and a player with four of five Rings who is one condition short.

## 7. Reactions

**The idea:** a Reaction answers something that just happened. Several things can answer the same event, and there is an order.

- **What a Reaction is.** An ability taken in response to a trigger, written as "before", "after" or "when" an occurrence. Reactions are always voluntary.
- **Reading triggers.**
  - Indefinite plurals ("After Focus Effects resolve") name a point in time and do not need anything to happen at it.
  - Definite wording ("After a Focus Effect resolves") needs the thing to have happened.
  - "Would" wording ("before X would happen") means the same as "before X happens".
  - "After" one step happens before "before" the next step. It is in neither step.
  - "When" falls between "before" and "after". It is used for choices that alter a player's choice, and for conditions becoming true or false.
- **Taking a Reaction.**
  - Respond as soon as the trigger occurs. You cannot save it for later.
  - Once per turn by default. A single copy cannot be taken twice to the same trigger.
  - Cards in play and in the correct area only, unless the card says it works out of play (for example from your hand).
  - The Reaction round: starting with the active player, each player in turn takes a Reaction or passes. When all pass in a row, no more Reactions to those triggers.
- **The timing sequence, when several things trigger at once:**
  - A: delay, negation, prevention.
  - B: substitution.
  - C: other non-Reactions (triggered traits, delayed effects, rulebook effects such as the Honor loss for a dishonorable Personality).
  - D: Reactions.
  - Earlier steps can make later ones impossible. A Reaction to something that no longer happens is lost.
  - The active player chooses the order within A, B and C.
- **Interrupts and Engage are Reactions.** *(Mendon's ruling.)* An Interrupt is taken in the Reaction window that suits its text, usually "before an action's effects resolve", and refers only to the action that triggered it. An Engage ability is a Reaction taken at engagement; if it is printed on a Personality or attachment, that card must be present at the battlefield (R-064: Regions, Holdings and Strongholds name their own battlefield; Home, Absent and the listed exceptions are exempt). An Interrupt that creates an effect after its own card enters play can be used on the action that brought it in. **(recap Wateru)**
- **"Next time" and "first time" triggers.** Default to this turn. With "or", the first of two events is the one that triggers.
- **Leaving play.** A trigger about a card leaving play looks at its state before it left.
- **Rulebook Reactions the player already has.** Proclaim, Conquest, Naval Invasion (Engage).
- **Worth showing.**
  - One trigger answered by two players, to show the order.
  - A Reaction that is lost because an earlier step negated the trigger.
  - A "before" and an "after" at the same step.

## 8. The Imperial Favor

**The idea:** one object, held by one player, with three uses.

- **What it is.**
  - A single physical object per game, representing the good graces of the Court.
  - It starts uncontrolled. One player at a time may control it.
  - It can be taken from another player or from no one, through lobbying or other effects.
- **Lobby (to take it).**
  - Political Limited: if you have higher Family Honor than each other player, bow your performing Personality with Personal Honor 1 or higher: take the Favor.
  - Show the Honor comparison on the placards, and a Personality with 0 Personal Honor who cannot do it.
- **Using it.** Two Favor abilities, both Political:
  - Limited: discard the Favor and a card to draw a card.
  - Battle: discard the Favor and target an attacking enemy Personality to move him home.
- **Discarding it.**
  - Discarding as a cost means passing it from you to uncontrolled.
  - An effect that discards the Favor makes it uncontrolled whoever holds it.
  - Some cards use it as a cost ("Bow your Stronghold or discard the Imperial Favor").
- **Who can lobby.** Only a player with higher Family Honor than every other player. A player behind on Honor cannot take it that way, though other effects can still give it.
- **Imperial.** A descriptive keyword on Personalities, except that Journey's End Keep (Experienced) turns it into a Clan alignment. That Stronghold also starts the player with the Favor if no other player has an Imperial Stronghold. **(recap Keywords 1)**
- **Worth showing.** Lobbying, a Favor draw, a Favor move-home in a battle, and the opponent taking it by lobbying when ahead on Honor.
