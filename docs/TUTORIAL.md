# Tutorial (issue #935)

Play → Tutorial teaches the game by playing it, offline: you are the **pinned Lion deck** against the **pinned Crane deck** (`content/published/lion-crane-v1.json`), for the first turn and a half of a game (59 steps, ending on your destroyed Province). Illegal clicks are locked out.

## Lessons

Every lesson is a `TutorialLesson` (`TutorialLesson.cs`): a `TutorialSetup` (each side's clan, Stronghold, deck tops, Honor and cards at home, plus who goes first), its steps, and a `TutorialFoeScript` saying what the opponent does when a step lets it play (which Personalities it recruits with the Clan Discount, what else it recruits, which abilities it uses on which target). `TutorialLessons.All` lists them; nothing is locked. The Play → Tutorial menu lists every entry of `TutorialCurriculum` by track (the readable copy and the task checklist, #1035, are in the curriculum doc); an entry whose lesson is not built is a dimmed placeholder showing its brief. To add one, write its setup and steps and list it there. The lessons still to write are in [TUTORIAL_CURRICULUM.md](TUTORIAL_CURRICULUM.md).

## How it works

- **The real engine plays the lesson.** `MatchEngine.CreateTutorial(setup)` builds a prepared table (each side's pinned deck, with the cards the lesson meets put on top in the order it needs them) and stops before the first turn. Nothing in the rules changed.
  Lion has the higher starting Honor (7 against 6), so you go first; Crane, second, starts with the bowed Bamboo Harvesters.
- **The engine can be asked to stop.** `TutorialHoldAt(phase, player)` makes the engine stop when it has just reached the Straighten phase (after its cards straightened), the Events phase (after the Provinces were revealed) or the End phase (before the draw). `TutorialResume()` carries on. Tutorial-only; no rules change.
- **The words are in the script.** `src/L5R.Core/Tutorial/TutorialLessonOne.cs` is an ordered list of steps. A *note* has a button; a *move* names the one move it waits for (`Allow`), optionally the parameters it must have (`Accept`: the Gold sources, the unit equipped) and the provinces the table may offer (`OnlyProvinces`).
  What a step does to the table when it is done (`Then`) says when a hold is lifted and when Crane plays; highlights (`Highlight`: cards), `Glow` (the phase bar, the battle bar, your hand), `ShowDefinitionId` (a card held large in the middle of the table) and `Slow` (the presentations the step starts run four times as slow) are all per step.
- **`TutorialRunner` narrows and plays the script.** It switches off every option the step does not ask for (the table then shows them unavailable), refuses anything else before the engine sees it, and plays Crane's side. Crane only answers on its own: passes, defends the Province you attacked. Everything else it does (declining its attack, recruiting Daidoji Kenshi and the farms, Never Afraid) is something a step asks for in `Then`, so the lesson can stop between its plays.
- **Back.** The runner keeps every button and move you made. `Back()` starts the lesson again and plays all but the last of them: the engine is deterministic, so that is the scene as it was. The coach panel's *Back* button does this.
- **The client only presents it.** `MatchSession.StartTutorial` hands the table the runner's options; `TutorialCoachPanel` shows the note in a column at the left of the table; `CardTile.Pointed` pulses on the cards the note names; `TutorialGlowFrame` (`Main.TutorialStage.cs`) pulses around a part of the screen; the Show flow, the slow Province layout and the four-times-slower reveals are in `Main.TutorialStage.cs` and `Main.CardPlays.cs`. The Title menu entry is `TitleMenu.Tutorial`.

## Writing or changing the lesson

1. Pick cards from `TutorialCards.cs` (add constants there; the test checks every number the text quotes against the catalogue and the engine).
2. Add steps in order in `TutorialLessonOne.cs`. Quote only what the Emperor Edition core rules (`docs/rules/EMPEROR_EDITION_CORE_RULES.md`) say. Say which controls to click: the table's own buttons and cards are what a player uses.
3. Extend `tests/L5R.Core.Tests/TutorialTests.cs` and run `dotnet run --project tests/L5R.Core.Tests -- --filter tutorial` (headless, seconds).
4. Add the new move steps' clicks to `TutorialUiScripts` in `Main.Tutorial.cs` and run `bash tools/check-tutorial.sh`, which plays the whole lesson through the real controls.

## Where a finished lesson returns to

A lesson that ends (its last button) or is left from the table menu returns to the menu list it was started from: lessons of Level Up Your Play (or Building a Deck) return to that track's list, and The Basics, a track of one lesson, returns to the Tutorial track list. `TutorialCurriculum.TrackOf` decides it; every new lesson inherits the behaviour by being listed in the curriculum. A step's `animateOnly` limits which cards' reveal animations play while it is showing (the Events lesson's Celestial step animates only Suitengu's Blessing).

`TutorialLessonDuels.cs`, lesson id `special-duels`: Crane (you, Shinden Asahina, 11 Honor) against Scorpion (Law of Darkness Dojo). The runner has no duel script of its own: a step's `Then` makes the opponent's duel moves with `TutorialContext.FoeDuel` (`declineDuel`, `focus` of a hand card or `choice: "deck"`, `strike`) and `FoeDecide("delay")` answers The Loyalty of the Scorpion's question, while `AutoSelectBattle` chooses the battle at the Province you attacked. The client shows the real duel screen for the lesson's duels and keeps a second coach card on its left (`Main.TutorialDuel.cs`); a step that needs the table sends the player back to it once the duel's result has been read, and Back closes an open duel. The lab's `--tutorial-auto` takes the one move a step with no scripted clicks offers.

## The Cost and Effect lesson (issue #1288)

`TutorialLessonCostEffect.cs`, lesson id `cost-effect`: Scorpion (you, The Towers of the Yogo, 2 Honor) against Dragon (Monastery of New Thought, 8 Honor), from your Action phase through one attack on the Dragon's Province 1. Notes on costs, effects and the two colons; Mysterious Deaths (its 6 Gold is its only cost; it destroys your Ninja and Togashi Meiyu, leaves the game and takes you from 2 to -2 Honor), paid without The Merchant's District; the District draws At Any Cost; you attack with Shosuro Koshiba (Nightingale Blade) and Bayushi Kasumi, Mirumoto Gobashi defends, his Khalimpeh-jiak (-2F) and Unparalleled Sword (Melee 4) destroy Koshiba; Revenge, performed by Kasumi, bows and destroys the Sword; Kasumi's Towers ability bows Gobashi; At Any Cost brings Koshiba back with the Clan Discount and you win the battle. The Ninja is made in the opening by the Overlooked Palace (`TutorialContext.Use`).

Corrections to the original script, checked against the Comprehensive Rules, the Emperor printings and the rulings (agreed with Mendon, 2026-11-18):
- The constraints block is not "the cost": it holds triggers, conditions, targets, performers and costs; only what tells you to do something (bow, pay, lose Honor) is a cost.
- Mysterious Deaths targets the unit with the lowest total Gold Cost, attachments included, not the lowest Personality. Targeting is not an effect (CR, Definition of effects), so it has three effects, not four; a player with no target simply has nothing destroyed.
- The Merchant's District's Emperor printing is "Limited: If you have four or fewer cards in your hand and you are a Scorpion Clan player, bow this card: Draw a card." The hand size and Clan are a condition; the cost is bowing it.
- At Any Cost's Emperor printing is "Battle: If you control any units at the current battlefield, choose your performing dead Samurai: Bring him into play at the current battlefield, paying all costs." Presence and the dead Samurai are a condition and a performer, not costs; the action has no costs. The Samurai's Gold Cost is the cost of bringing him into play (CR, Optional costs: required under the Good Faith Rule, but not a cost of the action). The later Oracle wording ("Dark Virtue Battle, [Gold]: Recruit your target ...") is not used.
- Khalimpeh-jiak's is an Engage ability (counted as a Reaction at engagement, ruling of 2026-10-31); its bow symbol is the later shorthand for "Bow this card".
- The Dragon destroy Koshiba (with his Nightingale Blade), not a Sasumata, and it is your turn, not theirs.
- Revenge's condition is "since the end of your last Battle action this battle", not "since your last turn". Choosing a performer and a target are not costs, so Revenge has no costs (its Gold Cost is 0); its bow and destroy are independent effects.
- The script's questions ("What are the costs and what are the effects?") are answered in the same step, as Mendon chose.

Engine difference the lesson states: costs are paid as the action is chosen, before it is announced. Engine note for later: `at-any-cost-recruit` reads "recruit your target performing dead Samurai"; the Emperor printing chooses a performer and does not target.

## The Rings and Enlightenment lesson (issue #1084)

`TutorialLessonRings.cs`, lesson id `rings`, the last lesson of Level Up Your Play (after Imperial Favor, at Mendon's request). Dragon Enlightenment (you, Pillars of Virtue, 9 Honor, Provinces 1-3 destroyed, Togashi Osawa in the last) against Scorpion (The Towers of the Yogo, 2 Honor, everyone bowed), late in the game in your Action phase. Four Rings are in play and the Ring of Fire is in your hand with Nerve Strike, Grappling the Snake and Deadly Discipline; seven cards are in your Fate discard pile. Notes show each Ring and its entry condition (Emperor printings) and what an Enlightenment Victory needs; Togashi Hizumi attacks alone, gives the province -4 strength (7 to 3), destroys it, and the Ring of Fire enters play. The last step, `rg-survive`, is played out (`TutorialStep.FreePlay`): every move is the player's, the client's Hard practice opponent plays the Scorpion, and the lesson ends when the match does. A win (by Enlightenment as your next turn starts, if you survive) shows "CONGRATULATIONS! YOU HAVE COMPLETED ALL OF THE TUTORIALS!" with only Tutorial Menu; a loss shows "YOU LOSE! TRY AGAIN!" with Restart Tutorial, which starts the lesson again. Back is not offered while the game is played out. The decks are filler made of the Dragon Enlightenment deck's own cards (no static deck was needed). `TutorialSide` gained `FateDiscard` and `DestroyedProvinces` for this table.

Corrections to the script, checked against the Comprehensive Rules and the Emperor printings:
- Enlightenment: the CR (The Player, Enlightenment Victory) asks for five Rings with different element keywords, each of which last entered play by its own text, checked after the start of one of your turns. A Ring put into play by another card does not count while it stays in play (it can count again once it leaves and re-enters by its own text).
- Ring of the Void: "your fourth or later non-Kiho action from Strategies in one phase": actions resolved from Strategy cards, not Strategies played. A Strategy action can be a Reaction or Limited action, so other phases are possible, but in practice it is the Action or Battle phase.
- Ring of Earth: "if there were ever any enemy units at that battlefield": the enemy must have had a unit there at some point in the battle, not necessarily opposing at the end.
- Ring of Air: the three Spell or Kiho actions must come from cards with different titles.
- Ring of Water: four Battle actions in one battle, from cards with different titles (not any four actions).
- Ring of Fire: the battle must be at a province; destroying a province or enemy cards in its resolution qualifies, so an unopposed win that destroys the province does too (the lesson's own path), as well as a tie or a win in an opposed battle.
- Several Rings from one effort: four non-Kiho Strategy actions and three Kiho actions in one battle, all different titles, meet the Void, Air and Water conditions at once (seven Battle actions).
- The script's step 14 ("pass ... and you'll be given the opportunity to play Ring of Fire") is two steps: pass, then put the Ring of Fire into play. The attack also needs Declare an attack and choosing the battle.
- Added a note on the Rings' own abilities (bow it or discard it from hand), which help the defence the lesson ends with, and on Rings staying in play when bowed.

Engine fixes made alongside: actions that end in a deck search (Martial Instruction) count toward the Rings' entry conditions; a Battle action between battles no longer crashes the action query (issue #1294).
