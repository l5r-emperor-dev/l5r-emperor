## 2. The goal, precisely

**Primary:** Conan, with a settings set you propose as its new defaults, wins **at least 80% of decided games against the Contender** (`L5R_TOURN_OPP=c2` with the Contender's default rules) on a **fresh seed block it has never been tuned on**, with 1,456 games (`L5R_TOURN_SEEDS=4`), pairing `independent`, mode `dev`, and then holds at least 75% on `holdout` mode (720 games) with the same settings. Both runs are pre-registered before they start.

**Guards that must also hold at the end:**

- Zero soundness findings beyond the Contender's own (stalls, illegal commands, exceptions, decision cap, killed at the cutoff).
- No deck Conan pilots at under 50% where it pilots at least 20 games.
- **Against the frozen opponent pool** (section 4, Tier 2) Conan does not lose ground: at least its merged 77% against the Champion, and no earlier Conan version beats the final one by more than net −10 on 364 paired games. The sparring partner must not become its only strength (Lanctot et al. 2017 on policies overfitting to their training opponents; Balduzzi et al. 2018 on why a single win rate hides cycles).
- Thinking time reported for every candidate: per search median, p90, p99, max, and per game mean and max (`tools/bots/think_times.py`). Mendon tolerates long thinking in human play ("most games take 20 to 30 minutes"), so a per-decision p99 up to about 15 s and a per-game mean up to about 3 minutes are acceptable in the final settings, but every result states its cost so he can choose. Arena throughput is the real constraint on how much you can test.

Secondary goals, in order: the same win rate at lower thinking time; fewer abandoned searches; a decision test set that runs in seconds; a Conan whose play on Mendon's replay positions agrees with him more often than the Contender does.

## 4. Testing method: the iterative adversarial loop

The loop has four tiers, cheapest first, and an adversarial pool that grows as Conan improves. Never promote a change on a cheap tier alone, and never run an expensive tier on an idea that has not passed the cheap one. The design follows what works in engine development (Fishtest's SPRT acceptance, paired games on shared openings, puzzle suites as regression checks: Chess Programming Wiki "Sequential Probability Ratio Test", "Engine Testing", "Test-Positions") and in multi-agent training (a league of frozen past versions and exploiters rather than one sparring partner: Vinyals et al. 2019; Lanctot et al. 2017).

### Tier 0: the decision test set and the oracle (build these first, in the first session)

1. **Decision test set.** Build `tests/L5R.AI.Tests/ConanPositions.cs` (reuse the `human-compare` replay machinery) so that a saved set of positions can be re-decided by any bot configuration in seconds, printing the chosen action and the search's values per candidate. Sources for positions: (a) Mendon's seven replays (section 6), where his choice is known and the Contender's differed; (b) the decisions in Conan's lost games that the oracle (next item) grades as errors; (c) positions where the 10-second limit abandoned the search. Each position records the replay or seed, the deck pair, the version, and the expected choice where known. A candidate change runs here first: it must not flip known-good decisions, and the number of known-bad decisions it fixes is its Tier 0 score. This is your inner loop; iterate here many times per hour. Remember the chess lesson that puzzle scores do not equal playing strength (Chess Programming Wiki, "Engine Testing"); Tier 0 filters ideas, it never confirms them.
2. **The oracle.** A diagnostic configuration that searches with the real match instead of redealt samples (a sampler that returns `Clone()` without redeal), with a larger budget (`lkK` 6, `lkN` 1 is enough because there is nothing hidden). It is a "cheater" (Świechowski et al. 2018 trained their value network on exactly such full-information games). Use it two ways, never as a player: (a) **grading losses**: replay each lost game, and at each of Conan's searched decisions compare Conan's value of its choice with the oracle's best; the decisions with the largest regret are the errors to study, which turns 105 lost games into a ranked list of mistakes with a one-line cause each (`tools/bots/mine_losses.py`); (b) **labelling training data** for the value function in section 5, B5.
3. **Decision categories.** Every mined error is tagged by phase and kind (attack shape, defence assignment, battle action order, Favor use, purchase, discard, Open-phase use, reaction, duel) so the experiment notes can say which category each change targets and how many tagged errors it fixed.

### Tier 1: paired screens on fresh blocks

364 games on a **block no arm has used before** (take new blocks from 7200 upward in steps of 100; record each block's use in the experiment note), `independent` pairing, `L5R_TOURN_OPP=c2`, `L5R_TOURN_BUDGET=20`, compared with the current best Conan settings on the same games via `compare_tournament.py`. A screen decides only what goes to Tier 2: take a change forward when it is net +8 or better with no soundness finding and no deck collapse. It proves nothing. Run at most three arms at once on the 10-core Mac; more than that distorts thinking times and pushes games into the cutoff (chess testing guides insist on one game per core for the same reason). If Mendon's PC is available, split blocks across the two machines as `docs/bots/CONTENDER.md` describes.

For numeric knobs (weights, `lkN`, `lkK`, margins), do not screen one value at a time. Use **successive halving** (Hyperband, Li et al. 2017): 8 to 12 configurations at one seed (182 games each), keep the best third, give them two more seeds, keep the best, then a full screen. For continuous weights, CLOP (Coulom 2011) or Stockfish's SPSA (Chess Programming Wiki, "Stockfish's Tuning Method") is the standard; CLOP is the better fit for a handful of weights on win/loss outcomes, and SPSA needs tens of thousands of games, which Conan cannot afford. Tune at most five parameters at once, and tune at cheap search settings first (`lkN` 2) so the loop turns over quickly, then confirm at the real settings.

### Tier 2: decisive runs with a sequential stopping rule, against the pool

For a change that passed Tier 1, pre-register and run on a fresh block with a **sequential probability ratio test (SPRT)** on the paired flipped games, as Fishtest does for Stockfish patches, instead of a fixed 1,456-game sample. This is a proposed amendment to the fixed-sample rule in `docs/bots/README.md`; record it as a decision in `docs/decisions/` with the justification (Conan games cost 4 to 5 times a Contender game; a sequential test with fixed error rates spends games only where the answer is close). The concrete rule:

- Statistic: among games whose outcome changed between the arm and the current best on the same seeds, the count gained (G) and lost (L). Under H0 the arm is no better (p0 = 0.5 of a flipped game being a gain); under H1 it is better by the registered effect (p1 = 0.6, roughly net +10 games per 50 flipped).
- Log-likelihood ratio after each 182-game chunk (one seed over all pairs, both seats): LLR = G·ln(p1/p0) + L·ln((1−p1)/(1−p0)). Accept H1 at LLR ≥ ln((1−β)/α), accept H0 at LLR ≤ ln(β/(1−α)), with α = β = 0.05 (bounds ±2.94, the same as Fishtest's). Stop at whichever bound is hit, or at a hard cap of 1,456 games (then record inconclusive). Analyse only at chunk boundaries, never mid-chunk, and never change the settings of a running test.
- Fishtest's pentanomial model treats the two games of a pair (both seats, same seed) as one trial because they are correlated; with `independent` pairing the two games have different shuffles, so the binomial on flipped games is adequate. If you ever switch to `same` pairing, count pairs, not games.
- Soundness, deck-collapse and thinking-time guards are checked on the full sample at the stop.

Write `tools/bots/sprt.py` to compute this from the JSON reports, and run batches chunk by chunk so a test can stop early.

**The frozen pool.** Keep a registry of opponents in `BotTournament.Arms` and `docs/bots/POOL.md`: the Champion, the Contender, each Conan version that was ever the "current best" (frozen as a named rule set), and every adversary deck from the Adversary role in `docs/bots/README.md`. A Tier 2 pass against the Contender is followed by a 364-game check against the pool's two most recent Conan versions (the arm in both seats). A change that beats the Contender by exploiting it, and loses to the previous Conan, is recorded, not promoted. Report each candidate's results as one row per pool member, not as one number; that is the cheap form of the AlphaStar league's lesson.

**Exploiters.** Every two Tier 2 passes, run one exploiter round: (a) the three held-out decks in both seats against the current best, and (b) the Adversary role: read only public information, build at most two decks or scenarios that target a visible habit of the current best (for example a deck that never commits Cavalry against Conan's attack shapes, or an Honor deck that punishes its Favor use), and run them on 182 games. An exploit that wins consistently becomes a Tier 0 position set and a hypothesis, never a named-card rule.

### Tier 3: confirmation

The final proposed default set runs the primary test in section 2 (fresh block, 1,456 games, then holdout 720, both pre-registered, fixed sample, no peeking) plus the pool guard. Only this run supports the claim "Conan reaches 80%".

### Statistical hygiene throughout

- Same seeds for every arm in a comparison; one deterministic game per (deck pair, seed, seat). Common random numbers inside the search (every candidate sees the same redeals) stay as they are; Forge's simulation AI seeds its RNG the same way for the same reason. An A/A on a new block before using it (Contender vs Contender) is both the baseline and a determinism check.
- Report the Wilson interval of the win rate, the net and the exact sign test of flipped games, per-deck tables, median and p90 game length, the three victory reasons, and thinking-time quantiles. One table per arm, in the experiment note.
- Treat any arm that gains more than about 15% of games on one screen as suspect until it repeats on a second block; large effects from small changes are usually a bug (for example a search that silently reads something it should not, or a comparison that drops games).
- Before trusting a new metric or tool, run it on an A/A and on a known result (`vc-plan` against `vc-cv` should reproduce +30).

## 5. Hypothesis backlog, in the order to try them

Work through these in order unless the evidence from the oracle's error ranking sends you elsewhere: **the first real experiment is to mine the losses and let the top error categories reorder this list.** Each item is a separate opt-in rule or knob, a separate arm, a separate line in the note. Stop a thread when two screens on different blocks are both under +8.

**A. Spend the search budget where the literature says it pays.**

1. **Anytime search (`lkAny`).** When `lkT` runs out, decide on the samples already completed (they are paired, so a partial mean is still a fair comparison) instead of discarding the search and falling back to the Contender. Those abandoned searches are the complex positions where search should matter most. Budget per turn as well as per decision, as chess time management does: a turn with many Battle decisions should not spend 10 s on each.
2. **More worlds, not deeper worlds.** Cowling, Ward and Powley's Magic: The Gathering study found that extra budget is best spent on more determinizations rather than deeper search in each, with the best split in the range of 20 to 100 worlds. Conan uses 4. Once playouts are cheaper (item 3), screen `lkN` at 8, 12 and 16 with `lkK` fixed, then `lkK` at 4 and 5, and report win rate per second of thinking. Also screen adaptive allocation: after four samples, add samples only while the top two candidates are within one standard error of each other (racing, the mirror image of `lkDrop`).
3. **Profile and cut the forward model.** Profile a playout again after the quote memo (`dotnet-trace`, or stopwatches around `QueryActions`, `GetView`, `Execute`, `Clone`). The general-video-game-AI literature finds copying and advancing states to be the dominant cost of every statistical forward-planning method, so this is the highest-leverage engineering item: a cheaper clone and view for playout bots, action-list construction limited to the acting seat's phase, no re-validation in playouts of what the engine already checked. Prove with the listing hash that nothing changed. A 2× cheaper playout doubles every budget in this section.
4. **Reuse determinizations across one attack.** Draw the redeals once at the start of the attack and reuse the same samples for every search in it: cheaper, and paired across consecutive decisions.
5. **A weaker rollout policy.** The MTG study found that rollouts with a reduced rule set beat rollouts with the best expert rules, because a deterministic expert biases every world the same way. Screen two playout policies for both seats: the Contender with a small deterministic noise in its action scores (`lkNoise`, seeded from the sample so it stays reproducible), and the Champion. `lkOpp` (the Champion for the other seat only) was +1 against the Champion; test it again against the Contender, and test noise separately.
6. **Guard against strategy fusion.** PIMC overvalues lines that "work if they do not have it", because each world is solved as if the hidden cards were known (Frank and Basin 1998; Long et al. 2010). Two cheap guards to screen: (a) `lkRobust`: rank candidates by mean minus a fraction of the standard deviation across samples, so a line that wins big in two worlds and loses the Province in two is not preferred over a safe line; (b) `lkWorst`: ties broken by the worst sample. Information-set MCTS (Cowling, Powley, Whitehouse 2012) is the principled fix and is out of scope for this campaign unless the two guards both fail and the error ranking says fusion is the cause.

**B. Value the end state better (the evaluation function).**

7. **Learned terminal value (`lkVal`).** Fit a logistic regression (or a tiny gradient-boosted model, no GPU) that predicts the eventual winner from card-agnostic features available at the end of an attack: Provinces left each side and their strengths, units' Force and Gold Cost in play (bowed and unbowed separately), Honor each side and its distance to the 40-Honor win and the Dishonor threshold, hand sizes, Gold production, turn number, who is the attacker, who holds the Imperial Favor, Rings in play. This is Buro's logistic evaluation fitting and the Texel tuning method applied to a CCG, and the recipe Świechowski et al. used for Hearthstone (a small value model trained on full-information games, used to cut rollouts off at the turn end). Training data: decision snapshots from arena logs of Conan, Contender and oracle games (thousands exist under `artifacts/bots/logs-*`; add a snapshot export if the logs lack a feature). Validate on held-out seed blocks. Use the model's logit in place of the linear material score. This replaces the hand-set weights, the ±1000 win term, and the ad hoc scarcity and Honor-curve rules (`lkPS`, `lkHS`) by one calibrated function, and it is what lets the search see tempo and the clock. Keep the model as a small table of coefficients in `src/L5R.AI`, not a runtime dependency; retrain once per Tier 2 pass from the new games (Świechowski retrained from about 3,000 games per iteration).
8. **Tune the existing weights as a fallback.** If the learned value does not beat the linear one, tune `lkP`, `lkU`, `lkH`, `lkC` with CLOP or coordinate successive halving on Tier 1 screens, starting from the signs the learned model gives.
9. **Horizon.** Keep the end of the attack as the horizon; `lkFar` lost because a longer rollout adds noise. With a calibrated value at the horizon, retest one longer horizon (through the opponent's defence assignment of their next attack) once, and drop it if it does not win.

**C. Search more of the decisions that decide games, so Conan adapts instead of following rules.**

10. **Attack plan quality.** `lkPlan` is the biggest win so far. Extend `AttackShapes` with the card-agnostic shapes section 6.2 asks for: "each unit alone at a Province it beats" (his split attacks), "declare although printed Force is short, counting hand Force and the Favor" (his turn-3 razes), "hold everything home", and the order of battles; then search the Cavalry declaration under the plan. Mine the 85 Military losses first: lost on defence (Provinces razed while units sat home) or on over-extension (units lost attacking)? Let the answer choose which shape to add first.
11. **Defence shapes.** `lkVar` gained a little; add "enough defenders that Force after my removals exceeds theirs" (defend to destroy), "one presence unit to unlock the hand", "defend only the Province whose loss would leave fewer than two", and "no defence, keep the hand and the bodies for the all-in", and let the search choose.
12. **Open-phase and Action-phase abilities.** Search the top three candidates at Action-phase decisions with a short horizon (to the end of the phase), with the learned value. Only after item 7, because a material score cannot see what an Open-phase play sets up (`docs/rules/BOT_PHASE_GUIDANCE.md` lists the once-per-turn budgeting problem).
13. **Purchases, again, with a value that knows economy and bodies.** `lkBuy` lost 10 games because a one-turn material score cannot see economy, and no purchase rule captured Mendon's Personality-first buying. Retry it only after item 7 with features that include Gold production, the next turn's affordable row and the count of unbowed Personalities; the horizon is the end of the bot's next attack. This is the single most likely place to capture his adaptivity.
14. **The Imperial Favor, reactions and duels.** Each is a small decision set; search each with the standard budget once the above are settled. Duels are answered differently by the arena bot and the client's `DuelOpponent`; note the discrepancy, do not fix it here.

**D. Robustness and fairness.**

15. **What the sampler knows.** `MatchEngine.Sample` reshuffles the real multiset of hidden card faces, so across samples the search effectively knows the opponent's decklist composition, which a human opponent would not reveal. This is accepted for bot-vs-bot measurement. Record it in `docs/bots/CONAN.md` under Limits and measure once how much it is worth: a `lkBlind` variant whose redeal draws the opponent's hidden faces from the cards the bot has seen plus the catalogue of that clan's legal cards. If the gap is small, the practice opponent can use the blind sampler later; if large, Mendon decides. Do not change the sampler's default.
16. **Adversary pass.** Before the final confirmation, run the exploiter round of Tier 2 one more time on the final settings and read the per-deck table for any deck that beats Conan consistently; mine its games with the oracle and treat the result as a Tier 0 position set, not as a reason to add a named-card rule.

## 6. Making Conan adaptive: what the rules and Mendon's games say

The aim is a bot that reads the situation and uses the cards it has, the way he does, not a longer list of rules. The mechanism for that is already in Conan: candidate generation, playouts that resolve the real cards, and a value at the horizon. Adaptivity is improved by (a) generating the candidates a strong player would consider, (b) valuing the end state by what matters in the current situation, and (c) testing against his positions. Each observation below names the mechanism it belongs to. **None of them becomes a fixed rule unless search cannot express it**, and then only as a card-agnostic, opt-in, measured rule.

### 6.1 Rules the search and the value function must respect

From `docs/rules/COMPREHENSIVE_RULES.md` and `docs/rules/RULINGS.md`; check each against the engine before relying on it:

- A Province falls only when attacking Force is **strictly greater** than defending Force plus Strength, and only on an attacker win at resolution ("Resolution Segment"; `MatchEngine.cs` uses `af > df + strength`). A tie razes nothing. Several of Mendon's "unnecessary" Force plays were exactly the point that turned a tie into a raze.
- Bowed Personalities and Followers contribute no Force but still die when the army loses; an unbowed Follower under a bowed Personality still counts, an Item does not. A side with no units has zero Force; an army of only bowed units against an empty province achieves nothing ("Total Force", "Bowed and unbowed").
- Attackers return home **bowed** and stay bowed through the opponent's whole turn; bowed units cannot assign, so attacking with a unit spends its ability to defend next turn. Surviving defenders stay at the battlefield **unbowed** ("Final Steps", "Straighten Phase"). This is the tempo cost a one-attack horizon does not see, and the first feature the learned value needs.
- The Defender has the first Battle action; the attacker assigns infantry first, the defender replies with full knowledge, then Cavalry on both sides in the same order; the attacker chooses the order of battles ("Combat Segment", "Maneuvers Segments", "Battles").
- The Rule of Presence: no Battle action without a unit at the battlefield, so one defender unlocks the hand; Reactions are exempt unless their text says otherwise (R-092); Open and Limited actions are never legal inside a battle (R-030).
- Resolution destroys the whole losing army and pays the winner 2 Honor per card destroyed, 1 per card in a tie; a destroyed dishonorable Personality costs its owner base Personal Honor regardless of immunities (R-083).
- Honor victory is checked at the start of a turn; Dishonor defeat is latched at −20 and delivered at that player's next End Phase ("Players winning and losing"). The clock features must use those timings.
- Lobby needs strictly higher Family Honor and bows a Personality; the Favor's Battle use moves an attacking enemy Personality home (about one Battle action, free); the Favor's Limited use cycles a card ("The Imperial Favor"). An Honor lead is therefore also a free send-home every turn.
- Holdings enter play bowed and produce nothing until the owner's next Straighten Phase; an in-clan Personality is a Gold-versus-Honor choice (discount, or Proclaim for base Personal Honor); a single Gold source cannot be split and excess is lost ("Gold production", "Clan alignment and entering play"). Discarding a face-up Province card ends buying for the Dynasty Phase: buy first, discard last.
- Three copies per title and all face-up zones public ("The play deck", "Game knowledge"): the opponent's Fate discard pile is a census of what remains, which `BotContext.ExpectedHiddenCopies` already models.
- `BattleOrOpen` abilities are once per turn; an Open-phase use forfeits the battle use (`docs/rules/BOT_PHASE_GUIDANCE.md`).

### 6.2 What Mendon does, and where it belongs in Conan

1. **He counts what the hand and the Favor add, not the printed Force.** In four of five Military wins he razed on turn 3 with a single unit below the Province's Strength by using Tactical Advantage (discard a card for its Focus Value) or a Force card; the Contender's plan log says "no attack this turn (my Force 3, theirs 0)" on those very turns. Conversely he declined attacks at 6 vs 7 and at 19 vs 15 when the defenders had unspent tricks. *Mechanism:* the attack search (`lkPlan`) must consider declaring even when the planner's printed-Force estimate says no, whenever hand Force (Tactician count × best Focus Values, Force cards by intent) plus the Favor could beat Strength; the playout then proves it. Interview: "If I think I can win the battle, I attack."
2. **He defends to destroy, not to hold.** Three defenders against two attackers, penalties aimed at Followers so resolution kills the Personalities, the Favor **held** until the opponent's tricks were spent, Costly Feud twice on one Paragon. The bot moves attackers home (Favor, Block Supply Lines) where he bows or shrinks them so that resolution destroys them. Notes: "keen to get the kill on him even if it meant the province didn't get destroyed." *Mechanism:* `DefenceShapes` gains "enough defenders that Force after my removals exceeds theirs" and the value function must prize enemy Personalities destroyed above a Province held when the clock allows; the Favor's value as a held resource is a feature (who holds it), so spending it must buy more than one send-home's worth.
3. **He splits attacks at an undefended home and probes with one unit when defenders exist.** Each unit at a Province it beats alone (games 3, 4, 6, 7, two razes in one turn), the single strongest unit alone as a probe, the rest home. The bot stacks everyone at one Province. *Mechanism:* `AttackShapes` already has "spread" and "probe"; add "each unit alone at a Province it beats", and search the **order of battles** (the attacker chooses it) when more than one battlefield has units.
4. **He declines fights he cannot win and keeps the army for the all-in.** Twice he parked defenders at unattacked Provinces against 13 to 14 Force with 5 to 6, let a Province fall, and razed the last Province the next turn with everyone. The bot assigns defenders where it is attacked and loses them a turn early. Interview: "If I cannot produce a win under any circumstance, I let the province fall." But when a defence unlocks hand actions that win, he commits many bodies (six against four). *Mechanism:* a "no defence, keep the hand and the bodies" shape, and a value that counts unbowed units at home for next turn and Province scarcity (losing the third Province is not three times the first; it is the one before the last).
5. **He spreads attachments and uses free cards first.** Three Blossoms on three Maidens, Followers on the body without a Sword, cheapest attachments first so dear Gold sources pay for dear cards. Already partly captured by `spreadwide`/`spreadvwide`. *Mechanism:* leave as rules; verify the search does not undo them.
6. **He buys Personalities before Holdings while he is ahead on bodies, and Holdings when he is not.** "As long as I was able to press the attack and kept coming home with an advantage in personalities and force ... I was happy for a weak economy ... if I hadn't had the early attack advantage, I'd have dug for holdings." Forcing Personality-first lost 41 games in the arena, because the choice depends on the situation. *Mechanism:* this is exactly what `lkBuy` with a value that sees bodies, tempo and Gold production (section 5, item 13) is for; the Gold-versus-Honor Proclaim choice belongs there too, read against the Honor clock.
7. **He keeps one-shot cards that scale with the opponent's investment** (a steal, a kill, a hold-the-attacker card) through many end-phase discards and sheds situational Force cards; the Contender's static rank proposed discarding the steal four times. *Mechanism:* a card-agnostic hand valuation by ability intent (destroy, steal, send home, negate above plus-Force) used at End-phase discards and in the value's card-in-hand term; search cannot play out a discard cheaply, so this is a rule plus a feature.
8. **He times the Favor by the situation.** He lobbies with the least useful unbowed unit, never with one he will attack with; in Honor races he cashes the Favor for Honor or Honor loss, not a draw; in battle he holds it until the end. Interview: "I would not lobby for the imperial favor when I'm down a personality on a turn I plan to attack." *Mechanism:* lobby performer choice is a rule (cheapest Force); Favor spending is a searched decision with the Favor as a value feature.
9. **He uses the opponent's turn.** Bowing their Gold Holding every turn to cut their purchases, straightening his own repeatable Holdings for reuse, spending the once-per-game Border Keep refresh only when the row is dead (the bot spends it on turn 1). *Mechanism:* Open-phase search (section 5, item 12) with the learned value; the refresh is a rule with a condition on the row's affordable value.
10. **He reads the opponent's hand size and seen tricks when sizing a defence or an attack**, and which enemy unit has already spent its printed action. The `conf` and triage rules read some of this; the playout reads none of it because the sampled hand is random. *Mechanism:* the value feature "opponent's hand size" and the sampler's census of seen copies; later, the `lkBlind` comparison in section 5, item 15.

### 6.3 The situation read

A strong player's context is a handful of public numbers. Compute them once per decision in `BotContext` and give them to the learned value and to candidate generation, instead of a fixed Attack/Defend/Develop intent:

- **Clocks:** my turns to 40 Honor at my current rate, theirs; my turns to raze their remaining Provinces at my unbowed Force against their defence, theirs against mine; distance of each side to −20. The shorter clock says whether to race or stabilise; the value function learns the trade.
- **Tempo:** unbowed Force at home after this attack against the opponent's possible attack next turn (their unbowed Force plus likely hand Force); who holds the Favor and whether Lobby is available next turn (Honor strictly higher).
- **Material and cards:** bodies in play each side, Gold production each side (Holdings enter bowed, so next turn's), hand sizes, cards seen in their discard (what tricks are exhausted).
- **Route:** my deck's route from my own decklist (legitimate self-knowledge; `DeckArchetype` exists), their route inferred from public cards (Followers and Battle Strategies seen, Political cards, Rings, average Personal Honor).

Let the search choose the intent by comparing shapes under the value, not by a rule; log the situation read in `PlanLog` so a reviewer can see what Conan thought it was doing.
